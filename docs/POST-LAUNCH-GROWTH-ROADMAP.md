# Post-Launch Growth Roadmap

The website is the foundation.

Getting the site right means Google can crawl it, understand it and trust the basic shape of the business.

Building authority means the market has a reason to talk about Essential Resourcing, cite David, link to the work and remember the point of view.

That is the bit that compounds.

## Strategy Summary

Technical SEO gets the doors open. It does not build the queue.

The next stage is founder-led market authority:

- Useful content that answers real hiring questions.
- Salary and market snapshots based on checked evidence.
- Digital PR angles journalists and trade audiences might actually care about.
- LinkedIn distribution that sounds like David, not a marketing department.
- External mentions, links and references that help Google, AI search and buyers trust the brand.

Do not chase algorithms. Build useful, first-hand market insight that the right people would read, cite, share and trust.

## Salary And Market Data Strategy

Salary content can become a serious authority asset, but only if it is honest.

Do not fake salary data. Do not invent research. Do not publish a table because the site needs a table.

Recommended assets:

- North West Senior Marketing Salary Snapshot.
- Manchester Agency Hiring Snapshot.
- PR & Communications Salary Snapshot.
- Digital and Performance Marketing Salary Snapshot.
- Strategic Interim Rate Guide.
- Senior Marketing Leadership Hiring Report.
- Agency Client Services Salary Snapshot.

Each asset needs:

- Data source notes.
- Review date.
- Role scope.
- Seniority and decision-rights context.
- Hybrid/location assumptions.
- Clear caveats.
- Related service links.
- CTA back to a real conversation.

How David can collect the data:

- Keep an anonymised monthly log of live briefs.
- Record salary ranges discussed with clients and candidates.
- Note why candidates say yes, no or maybe.
- Tag role scope: builder, operator, strategist, interim, fractional or permanent.
- Review small samples honestly rather than pretending they are national research.

Recurring pillars:

- The problem behind the hire.
- The job title is not the brief.
- No CV flinging.
- Senior marketing hiring mistakes.
- Strategic Interim explained.
- Agency hiring reality.
- Salary reality checks.
- Candidate market truth.
- Founder bottlenecks.

- One useful point of view post.
- One brief breakdown or anonymised lesson.

The posts should sound like David: plain, specific, human and commercially useful.

## Monthly Distribution Workflow

### Week 1

- Publish one cornerstone or market insight.
- Create three LinkedIn posts from it.
- Add internal links from the article to relevant service pages.

### Week 2

- Pitch one digital PR or expert comment angle.
- Offer David as an expert source where he can speak from experience.
- Repurpose the article into a short opinion post.

### Week 3

- Publish one shorter FAQ-led insight.
- Update one service FAQ from a real client question.
- Add a case study or proof point only if permission is clear.

### Week 4

- Review Search Console, analytics and LinkedIn performance.
- Review enquiries and CTA clicks.
- Choose next month's topic from real data, not guesswork.

## Step-By-Step Roadmap

### Week 0

- Set up Search Console, GA4 or Tag Manager and submit the sitemap.
- Validate schema, robots, metadata and indexability.
- Check PageSpeed and Core Web Vitals.
- Confirm all tracking links work.
- Prepare the first four content pieces before promoting the site.

### First 30 Days

- Publish four cornerstone articles.
- Publish one salary snapshot only if data is verified.
- Post on LinkedIn three times per week.
- Pitch one expert comment or digital PR angle per week.
- Add one real case study if permission and outcome are clear.
- Review Search Console weekly.

### Days 31-90

- Publish two useful articles per month.
- Publish one market or salary snapshot per month only where data is good enough.
- Run one lightweight data-led PR campaign.
- Secure three to five relevant external mentions or links.
- Add two case studies if proof is verified.
- Improve pages using Search Console query data.

### Months 3-6

- Build a salary hub once enough validated data exists.
- Build a Strategic Interim resource centre.
- Publish a quarterly marketing hiring report if the evidence is strong enough.
- Run a founder-led PR or expert comment campaign.
- Create repeatable monthly reporting.

### Months 6-12

- Create an annual market report or proprietary survey.
- Run a founder panel or roundtable.
- Build a stronger media list.
- Turn the best insights into guides.
- Refresh older content.
- Track enquiries by content source.

## Implementation Notes

### Site And CMS

Do not overbuild the CMS yet.

The current CMS already supports the first phase through insights, salary snapshots, case studies, FAQs, CTAs, proof items and SEO fields.

Future additions, when there is real use for them:

- External coverage field or document type.
- Report/download content type.
- Source/citation field for data-led content.
- Media bio / quote bank for David.
- Newsletter signup and archive if David commits to sending one.
- Open Graph image templates for reports and campaigns.

### Manual Research Needed

- Salary ranges from real briefs and candidate conversations.
- Hiring timeline examples.
- Agency hiring pressure points.
- Candidate expectation themes.
- Permissioned case study outcomes.
- Media, podcast and newsletter target list.

### Risks

- Publishing fake salary data would damage trust.
- Overbuilding a content machine David cannot run would waste effort.
- PR ideas without evidence will feel gimmicky.
- Too much generic thought leadership will weaken the David Walsh tone.

## Final Principle

No faff.

No fake data.

No SEO sludge.

No recruitment nonsense.

Build useful, first-hand market insight that the right people would actually read, cite, share and trust.
