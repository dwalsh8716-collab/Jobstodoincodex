## Claims Unsafe To Publish

1. **Unsupported sample counts and false precision.** The supplied brief gives per-row counts and claims rigorous deduplication without a row-level vacancy register. Its examples do not substantiate those totals. Remove the counts, statistical confidence language and assertions of proven regional medians/ceilings until the underlying records and method exist. Several syndications can represent the same job.
2. **Misclassified source used to force media salaries down.** Black Cherry is the decisive category error described above. A London Salary.com page is also not primary evidence for a Manchester median. The claimed GBP 10,000-15,000 creative-to-media pay drop, extreme network title inflation and independent/network pay hierarchy remain unsubstantiated, not established market facts.
3. **False technology premise.** The brief claims complete third-party-cookie deprecation as a cause of analytics demand. Google's 22 April 2025 announcement retained Chrome's existing cookie-choice approach and dropped the new standalone prompt. Delete the complete-deprecation assertion and any causal salary argument built on it. [Google primary announcement](https://privacysandbox.google.com/blog/privacy-sandbox-next-steps).
4. **Unverified role substitutions.** SEO Manager is not automatically an agency Strategist; Data Security Manager is not Marketing Analytics Manager; a government Senior Insight Manager is not automatically commercial Consumer Insight Manager. The supplied MHCLG GBP 49,256 and Liverpool customer-analytics GBP 54,000 claims were not independently verified against identifiable primary adverts in this check. York is a Northern comparator, not NW evidence.
5. **Sweeping trends without a suitable dataset.** Claims that Planners are statistically disappearing, that town-planning data demonstrably depresses marketing salary medians, that remote premiums have receded, or that client-side benefits/hours are consistently better need evidence beyond assorted adverts. Filtering unrelated planning jobs is sensible; claiming a measured market distortion is a different proposition.
6. **Useful distinctions presented as absolute rules.** Separate research-led insight from technical analytics where remits differ, but do not claim an impermeable split or a quantified skills premium from unlike jobs. RGML's mixed remit is a direct counterexample to rigid boundaries. The Forward Role creative advert does not establish the asserted Senior Strategist-to-Strategy Director pay jump.
