# Essential Resourcing Service Pages

Pre-architecture copy snapshot. This captures the wording before the latest four-product brief was applied locally. For the current direction, see [Services architecture working draft](./SERVICES-ARCHITECTURE-WORKING-DRAFT.md).

Copy and page-structure reference for the four service pages. The service-specific wording below is taken from the site's earlier public service content; all four pages use the shared `/services/[slug]` layout. Related proof and insight cards are rendered from published content, so those cards may change as content is published.

## Shared Page Layout

Each service page follows this order:

1. Breadcrumb: Services → current service.
2. Dark hero: service name eyebrow, H1, supporting paragraph, primary contact CTA and WhatsApp CTA.
3. Three-card overview: Who it is for / Audience; Problem / What it solves; Use case / When this makes sense.
4. Muted market-fit section: short label, heading and supporting market summary.
5. Process section: process label, heading, service-specific steps and a link to [How I recruit](/how-essential-resourcing-works).
6. Service-judgement section: service-specific eyebrow and heading, followed by short paragraphs explaining David's approach.
7. Public LinkedIn recommendations relevant to that service, where available.
8. Common mistakes: shared heading and service-specific mistake cards.
9. Related proof, related services and related insights, only where published/relevant items exist.
10. Service FAQs.
11. Final CTA section with service-specific heading and copy, a contact CTA and WhatsApp.

The service pages are concise commercial pages, not separate long-form process guides. Their detailed recruitment methodology links to `/how-essential-resourcing-works`.

## Retained search process

**Heading:** Not more CVs. A proper search.

1. **Interrogate the brief** — Why are you hiring? What needs to change? What evidence actually matters?
2. **Map the market** — Relevant businesses, adjacent sectors and senior people who have done something genuinely comparable.
3. **Approach passive candidates** — Strong people need proper context and a reason to listen, not a lazy message.
4. **Get behind the CV** — What did they actually deliver, how did they lead and what was genuinely theirs?
5. **Shortlist with evidence** — A considered shortlist with strengths, questions, motivation, salary and concerns.
6. **Keep the whole search moving** — Candidate management, feedback, offers and straight conversations from start to finish.

## What is a Fractional marketing leader?

**Heading:** A Fractional marketing leader is an experienced senior marketer who joins a business for a defined period or number of days to lead, advise and help deliver against an agreed commercial problem.

**Summary:** Unlike traditional consultancy, they're typically embedded in the business and accountable for helping make things happen, not just recommending what somebody else should do.

## Fractional process

**Heading:** Senior capability, just not necessarily five days a week.

1. **Start with the outcome** — What does the business need this person to fix, build, lead or change?
2. **Work out what level you actually need** — CMO? Marketing Director? Agency MD? Specialist leader? One day a week or three? Don't overhire the title.
3. **Find somebody who's done it before** — Relevant experience matters because an interim needs to get useful quickly.
4. **Get clear on scope** — Agree priorities, access, decision-making and what success should look like.
5. **Review impact** — The point isn't to fill somebody's diary. It's to make progress against the reason they were brought in.

**Process link:** See exactly how I recruit → `/how-essential-resourcing-works`

## Fractional vs interim vs consultancy

**Heading:** Don't get too hung up on the label. Get clear on the problem.

Fractional means senior experience for the amount of time the business actually needs.

That might be one day a week, three days a week, a defined interim period or support while you work out the permanent structure.

The right leader gets properly into the business. They don't drop in once a month, produce a PowerPoint and vanish.

## Questions clients ask

**What is a Fractional marketing leader?**

A Fractional marketing leader is an experienced senior marketer who works with a business for a defined number of days or period to provide leadership, direction and practical support.

**Is Fractional the same as interim marketing leadership?**

Sometimes. Fractional often means part-time senior leadership. Interim usually means a defined temporary period. In reality, the right model depends on the business problem.

**How is Fractional different from consultancy?**

Consultancy is often advisory. A good Fractional leader gets closer to the business, helps lead the work and stays involved when decisions meet reality.

**Which roles can be filled on an interim or fractional basis?**

Common roles include Interim CMO, Interim Marketing Director, Interim Head of Marketing, Interim Growth Director, Interim Digital Director and senior agency leadership.

**How many days a week does a Fractional leader usually work?**

It depends on the problem. Some businesses need one day a week, others need two or three. The right answer comes from the outcome, not the title.
