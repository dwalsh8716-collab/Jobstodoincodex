# Visual Design Polish

This is the final design polish record for the Essential Resourcing site.

The goal was not to redesign the site. The goal was to make the existing premium, founder-led direction feel more deliberate, more robust and more finished.

## Audit Summary

### What Already Looked Strong

- The homepage has a clear, memorable proposition.
- The graphite, porcelain, cobalt and copper palette feels distinctive and more premium than a generic recruitment theme.
- The type pairing gives the copy enough weight without feeling cold.
- Service, insight, job and proof cards already share a clean component language.
- The media fallback avoids fake founder imagery and does not rely on stock portraits.
- Forms are direct, useful and consistent with the David Walsh tone.
- Draft proof and unvalidated salary content are treated honestly.

### What Felt Generic Or Unfinished

- Cards needed a stronger top-level visual signature and better focus states.
- Footer links and contact routes felt more functional than finished.
- Some type sizes scaled with viewport width, which made long headings harder to control.
- Forms, media blocks and salary tables needed the same premium surface treatment as the main cards.
- The homepage needed one sharper editorial point-of-view moment before the service grid.
- The optional Manchester palette was still advertised in docs, which conflicted with the current brand direction.

### What Was Improved

- Kept the graphite, cobalt and copper palette as the production direction.
- Removed the Manchester palette option from the active token system and design docs.
- Replaced viewport-scaled font sizes with controlled type steps and responsive breakpoints.
- Added stronger card hierarchy with a restrained top accent rule, better hover and focus states.
- Refined buttons, text links, forms, salary tables, rich media and images with consistent shadows and transitions.
- Improved the header CTA and mobile navigation bounds.
- Strengthened the footer with clearer brand language and contact signals.
- Added a homepage editorial statement: "The job title is not the brief."
- Added reduced-motion safeguards for users who prefer less animation.

### What Was Deliberately Left Unchanged

- No fake testimonials, fake logos, stock founder portraits or made-up proof were added.
- The homepage structure, CMS architecture, forms and SEO system were preserved.
- The current colour palette was not replaced.
- The brand remains blunt, warm and commercially sharp rather than decorative.

## Page-Level Summary

### Homepage

- Stronger editorial flow after the proof strip.
- More deliberate surface and card detail.
- Existing founder/video/proof approach preserved until real assets are available.

### Service Pages

- Shared service cards now feel more premium across all routes.
- Related proof and related insight sections benefit from the stronger card system.
- No service-specific gimmicks were introduced.

### Insights

- Article typography is more predictable.
- Pull quotes and related cards now sit inside a more polished visual system.
- The editorial feel is improved without changing the content model.

### Jobs

- Job cards, forms and closed-role states use stronger surfaces and focus treatment.
- Live/closed/draft integrity remains untouched.

### Case Studies

- The proof-standard cards now feel more intentional.
- Draft proof remains clearly draft and is not dressed up as verified client evidence.

### Contact

- Form surfaces, focus states and assurance copy now feel more finished.
- Contact routes in the footer make the next step easier to find.

### Header And Footer

- Header CTA is more confident.
- Mobile nav has safer height handling.
- Footer now feels like a proper brand endpoint, not a loose link dump.

### Mobile

- Long headings use fixed responsive type steps rather than viewport scaling.
- Cards, buttons and mobile navigation have more stable sizing.
- Reduced-motion support keeps interaction polish accessible.

## Accessibility And Performance Safeguards

- Contrast-sensitive palette tokens were kept from the previous pass.
- Focus states remain visible.
- Motion is subtle and disabled for reduced-motion users.
- No new heavy libraries or client-side design dependencies were added.
- Visual changes are CSS and small markup changes, so performance impact stays low.

## Future Design Recommendations

### Immediate Before Launch

- Review final homepage, contact and footer screenshots on the production URL.
- Add the real David portrait if approved.
- Add final video poster imagery if the video goes live.
- Check final contact details and LinkedIn link.

### First 30 Days

- Build one reusable case study visual pattern once the first permissioned proof is available.
- Add editorial image guidance for insights.
- Review real user paths from homepage to contact.

### Three Months

- Commission founder and workplace photography if the site is generating the right enquiries.
- Create branded social preview templates for insights and salary snapshots.
- Build a small report/guide visual system only when there is real content to justify it.

### Six Months

- Turn strong content themes into campaign page templates.
- Add event, roundtable or media-page design only if those activities are happening.
- Expand visual proof carefully, with permission and commercial relevance first.
